An editorial hello.

"Caio" reads like the Italian ciao — hello, goodbye, and everything in between. The wordmark is set in Playfair Display 900 — a high-contrast serif with weight and personality that feels editorial, confident, and quietly Nigerian-modern (think of how brands like Bottega Lagos or Patty Cake set their type). All four letters share the same weight and style; the "i" is tinted in our dark brown (or crisp white on dark surfaces) as a small, ownable detail that gives the mark its personality.

02

What goes where.

A practical reference for anyone touching the brand — designers, printers, store staff, delivery partners, franchise teams. Caio's logo system has three forms (Primary Lockup, Reversed Lockup, Monogram). Knowing which to use is the difference between a brand that feels effortless and one that feels overworked.

The rule of thumb: use the Primary Lockup (or its reversed variant on dark surfaces) wherever there's room and you want people to read the name. Use the Monogram wherever space is tight, recognition is already established, or repetition would feel heavy-handed.
Application Use Why
Pizza box — lid Primary Lockup / Reversed The customer's hero moment. Brown kraft + white print. Wordmark front and centre.
Pizza box — side panels Monogram Small surface, repeated 4× around the box. Repeating the wordmark gets tired fast.
Pizza box sticker / seal Monogram Round seal that closes the box. The Ci is built for this.
Storefront — main signage Primary Lockup / Reversed The hero brand moment. Visible from the street, read at a glance.
Storefront — window decals / door Monogram Smaller surfaces, supporting role. Adds polish without repeating the main sign.
Menu — cover / front Primary Lockup / Reversed First touchpoint at the table. Show the full brand.
Menu — section dividers Monogram Quietly marks sections (pizzas, drinks, dessert) without re-announcing the brand.
Table tents / promo cards Primary Lockup / Reversed Customers read these while seated. They have time. Give them the full wordmark.
Apron — front / chest Monogram Embroidered chest mark. Wordmark would be too wide and read as a billboard.
Apron — back / tie Primary Lockup / Reversed More space, more room for a statement. Visible while staff plate.
Staff cap / beanie Monogram Small embroidery surface. Ci stitches cleanly; wordmark gets lost.
Delivery bike / scooter — main panel Primary Lockup / Reversed Big visible surface running through Lagos traffic. Free advertising — use it well.
Delivery bike — helmet / rider bag Monogram Smaller curved surfaces. The mark survives where the wordmark would distort.
Chowdeck / Glovo storefront tile Monogram Square thumbnail in a crowded grid. Only the monogram is readable at app size.
Website — header / nav Primary Lockup / Reversed First impression, plenty of room.
Website — favicon / browser tab Monogram 16×16px. Only the monogram survives.
Website — footer Primary Lockup / Reversed Reinforces the brand at end of page.
Instagram / TikTok — profile photo Monogram Circular crop at 40px. A wordmark can't survive that compression.
Social — post graphics / story templates Primary Lockup / Reversed Square or vertical canvas with room. Let people read the brand name.
Social — watermark on photos Monogram Small mark in the corner. Should sign the photo, not own it.
Business cards — front Primary Lockup / Reversed Standard professional pattern. Full brand on the front.
Business cards — back Monogram Decorative back. Adds polish without repeating the front.
Loyalty card / stamp card Both Wordmark at the top, monogram inside each stamp circle.
Flyer / promotional poster Primary Lockup / Reversed Marketing piece. Show the full brand.
Email signature Primary Lockup / Reversed Plenty of room. Reads cleanly in inbox preview.
App icon Monogram Square icon at small sizes. Wordmark is illegible here.
The pattern to remember

Big enough to read comfortably + first impression → Primary Lockup.
Smaller than a thumbnail OR confirming / decorative role → Monogram.

One useful design principle

Don't use both logos in the same composition unless there's a real reason (like a loyalty card). Pick one per surface, let it breathe. Using both at once makes the brand feel like it's trying too hard.

Four tones. Two heroes.

Dark brown and crisp white do the heavy lifting — a deep, confident, grown-up palette that stands out on a Lagos street full of red-and-yellow pizza signs. Two supporting tones (warm cream, bone) provide range without breaking the family. No accent color, no black — emphasis lives in italic serif and light/dark contrast, not in a spark.

How Caio talks.

Warm but never schmaltzy. Confident but never cocky. A touch of Naija English where it lands naturally — never forced. We trust customers to get the wink, whether they're regulars at the Lekki spot or first-timers.

Bold, not loud.
Short sentences. Confident verbs. The pizza speaks for itself — we don't have to oversell it.
Local, not parochial.
Suya and jollof on a wood-fired crust. We're proudly Nigerian; we just happen to make excellent pizza.
Curious, not pretentious.
We'll put dodo on a pizza. We won't lecture you about why — we'll just hand it to you hot.
Generous, not performative.
We warn you when it's actually spicy. We bring the bread before you ask. Small kindnesses, no spotlight.
